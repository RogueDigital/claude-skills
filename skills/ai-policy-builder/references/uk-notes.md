# UK notes the policy leans on

Each page below was opened and answered 200 on 21 October 2026. This is not legal advice. Send readers to the page, do not restate it as law.

## UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018

- **UK GDPR (legislation.gov.uk).** The main rules on personal data. Article 5(1)(c) is data minimisation: personal data should be adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary. This is why the policy says "only what the task needs" and "remove names if the task works without them".
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2016/679/contents
- **Data Protection Act 2018.** The UK law that sits alongside the UK GDPR.
  https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2018/12/contents

## ICO guidance

- **Guidance on AI and data protection.** The ICO's main AI guidance. It is under review following the Data (Use and Access) Act, so read the current version.
  https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/
- **Security and data minimisation in AI.** The ICO says to identify the minimum personal data you need for your purpose and only process that. Supports the amber rule.
  https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/artificial-intelligence/guidance-on-ai-and-data-protection/how-should-we-assess-security-and-data-minimisation-in-ai/
- **Personal data breaches: a guide.** A notifiable breach goes to the ICO within 72 hours of becoming aware of it, and delay must be explained. Supports the "tell us the same day" rule.
  https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/report-a-breach/personal-data-breach/personal-data-breaches-a-guide/
- **When do we need to do a DPIA?** A data protection impact assessment is needed where processing is likely to result in a high risk to people. If in doubt, the ICO recommends doing one. Point owners here when AI will run over customer records at scale or inform decisions about people.
  https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/accountability-and-governance/data-protection-impact-assessments-dpias/when-do-we-need-to-do-a-dpia/

## What these do not cover

Sector regulators, client contract terms and employment law. Those go to a solicitor.
